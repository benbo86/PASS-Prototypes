Current printout

Text sizes on the cards of the live customer schedule (A4 portrait, weekly), measured from a live PDF and compared with the original design. Sizes are in points as rendered, so the design figures are what the scaled card actually works out to, not the number typed on the component.

The design is several years old. The live PDF is what offices print and email today.

What it tells us

  • Live is close to the design, so the design isn't the reason it's small.The name is 5% smaller, the label matches, and only the time is larger (17%). The small text was designed in.
  • Everything on a card is under 9pt.The name is 8.25pt and the time and label are 6.75pt. The Large text criterion asks for a minimum of 16pt, about twice the name and 2.4 times the time and label.
  • The seven-column layout can't hold 16pt.Today's layout fits a whole week as seven day columns. At 16pt the columns are too narrow for names and times, so Large text needs a different layout, not just a bigger font.

Competitor research

A weekly schedule for one customer, produced by Nourish and shared with us by Care yourway, one of our customers, on 18 November 2026. Measured from the PDF file itself.

Nourish weekly client schedule A4 portrait

11.2pt for everything: the date range, customer name, dates, times, durations and carer names are all one size, in one font (DejaVu Sans, a wide typeface, so it reads larger than the same size in a narrower one).

A list, not cards: one row per day with the date, then the time and duration ("09:15 - 09:45 (30 minutes)"), then the carer. The customer name sits in a merged cell down the left side. The agency logo is at the top.

Plenty of space: one week for one customer fills less than half the page, so more text could fit.

No visit status: there are no booked, pending or cancelled markers and no key.

What it tells us

  • Their text is bigger than ours without being a large-print option.11.2pt is well above our 6.75 to 8.25pt, yet still below the 16pt minimum, and below the 14pt option we're showing alongside it.
  • A list gives text room that cards don't.It's the format that keeps the time, duration and name on one line at a larger size. It is different from our card style, which is the question for Product.

One example from one customer. We haven't seen how Nourish handles long names, many visits in a day, or landscape.
