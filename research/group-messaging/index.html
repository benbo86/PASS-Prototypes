Group messaging research

What customers are asking for when they request group messages: conversations with several carers where everyone can reply, and replies to broadcasts.

Last updated 19 November 2026. Part of the wider messaging discovery and requirement coverage. Linked from Staff Messaging (web) and Staff Messaging (mobile app).

Support cases

Salesforce cases asking for group messaging, found by searching case subjects and text on 19 November 2026. Nine requests from six customers, all raised since 15 October 2026, the weeks after Two-Way Messaging was released. All are still "New Request". Together the six customers pay £8,078.25 MRR (Total Contracted MRR from Salesforce, each customer counted once).

What we've heard so far

  • Broadcasts can't be replied to, and that's the most common complaintFour cases say it directly. Customers want to send one message to several carers and have everyone reply in the same thread, as with a group chat.
  • Some want group chat to replace WhatsAppTwo customers say they run package or team groups on WhatsApp today, and a third wants fewer external messaging apps. Their reasons are governance, information on personal devices, and communication records spread across systems. Others only want the convenience of one message and one thread.
  • Groups by package, area or tagOne customer wants groups built from care packages, areas or custom tags. Others only talk about selecting several recipients when starting a conversation.
  • Beyond textTwo customers also ask for file and image sharing in groups. This is covered by a separate ticket, so it isn't part of this one. One also asks for read status and acknowledgements to carry across, and another for an office-level on/off setting for group chats.

What this means for the prototypes

  • Two different needs sit under one phrase.Replies to a broadcast, and a true group chat where everyone writes to everyone. The cases use both words for both, but what they describe is the same thing: one conversation everyone can see and answer.
  • Broadcasts can't be replied to today.AIOP-23550 built broadcast threads as read-only with a "can't reply" notice, and a carer has to start a thread with the office themselves.

Proposal (for Product to confirm)

  • A broadcast has replies off, as today, or on.With replies on it is a group, shown with an icon like a broadcast. Recipients are chosen as today (employees, area or tag), and replies are off by default.
  • Created from its own menu item, not "All".Recipients are picked by employee (the default, with a select all option) or by area, and there is no "All" tab as for a broadcast. A group needs no name: if none is given it is called after its members, for example "Chloe Barker, David Chen, Emma Richardson and 2 others".
  • The office can add and remove employees.It can change the members at any time from the group's member list. When adding, the office chooses whether the new members see earlier messages (proposed default: no). Each change is noted in the conversation, a group always keeps at least one employee, and an unnamed group renames itself after its members.
  • Everyone sees everything in the group.Who sent each message, everyone's replies, and who is in the group.
  • Read status on your own messages only.Each person sees who has read the messages they sent, as in other messaging apps, and nobody sees it on other people's messages. This applies to office users and employees alike. The app side comes later, behind a feature flag.
  • The office turns groups on.It's a setting, off by default.
  • Web shows all groups, the app shows only yours.The web stays the shared inbox, and any office user can reply there. On the app an office user sees only groups they're a member of, with pushes and the unread badge following, as for 1:1s (AIOP-25726) and broadcasts (AIOP-25082).
  • Who is a member.The carers chosen, plus any office user who created the group or replied to it from the web. Replying makes them a visible member. Only reading a group on the web does not.
  • History on the app.An office user who is a member sees the full history, as they can on the web. Whether an employee added later sees earlier messages is the office's choice each time they add someone. Proposed default: no, as earlier messages may contain customer information.
  • First version.Groups are created on the web. A private reply linked to a broadcast, and groups that follow an area, tag or package as people move, can wait.

Not yet known

  • The word for groups.Whether "Broadcast" is shown anywhere on live, and what replaces it.
  • Full history on the app for an office user who joins by replying.For a 1:1, AIOP-25726 says messages from before the reply may not be shown, which looks like a limit of how the app syncs (for engineering).
  • What a removed employee sees.Whether the group disappears from their app and whether they keep the messages they already had, and whether an employee can leave a group themselves.
  • Data protection.Replies may mention customers, and members see each other's names.
  • Group size.A broadcast can already reach 200 or more people, and select all lets an office add everyone (for engineering).

Summarised from case descriptions written by our support team, not from conversations with the customers. We haven't searched Intercom conversations or calls.

Existing work

Searched Jira (AIOP) on 19 November 2026. There is no ticket for group conversations or replies to broadcasts.

  • Nothing covers group chat or replying to broadcasts.Of about 40 messaging tickets, none does. A ticket with unusual wording could have been missed.
  • Broadcasts are designed as read-only.AIOP-23550 (Accepted) built the read-only broadcast thread with a "can't reply" notice.
  • Nearby, not the same.AIOP-25608 "[PRODUCT TBC] Area access in messaging" (Backlog) is about who sees what by area. AIOP-25209 and AIOP-25208 (Backlog) let managers message carers and other managers from the app.
