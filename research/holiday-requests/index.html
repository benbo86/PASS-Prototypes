Customer discovery

Sessions with customers who saw the web and app prototypes. Quotes are lightly edited where the transcription garbled a word.

What we've heard so far

  • The office should decide what's deductedIssac said so unprompted: the app should only add the ability to request, with the office entering the amount as it does today.
  • Both flows are clearBoth customers found the web page and the app straightforward. OakShed called them "self-explanatory".
  • Responding quickly mattersOakShed must respond within a week; Issac's target is 48 hours and they hope the app gets it to 20–24.
  • The office checks the balance on approvalOakShed first wanted requests over the balance blocked, but agreed by email that the office checking on approval is fine. Issac would rather flag than block. The app blocks only at a zero balance.
  • Deductions come from contracted weekly hoursBoth work it out from the contract: OakShed spreads 37.5 hours over 5 days (7.5 a day), Issac takes 36 hours for a week on a 36-hour contract. Working days vary week to week at both, so the app can't work it out.
  • Approving less than asked for is commonOakShed does it often (their policy caps leave at 2 weeks, and they limit how many people are off at once); Issac does it occasionally. OakShed adds a note so there's a record.

Sessions

Issac

26 October 2026

Hours-based holiday schemes. Lots of independent supported living, with 12-hour and 24-hour shifts that include sleep periods.

How they work out deductions
  • From contracted weekly hours. A full week off on a 36-hour contract is 36 hours (7.2 a day).
  • Sleep periods earn holiday at a different rate, and cover and overtime change the picture week to week: "a minefield".
  • Working it out from availability, or from first and last calls, would take far too much: a home carer available 06:00–22:00 may only need 8 hours for the day.
"Don't let the AI or the app work it out… the only thing that would change would be the ability to apply for a holiday through the app."
Answers to our questions
  • Set days? Mostly, on rolling rotas, but they shift when someone covers a colleague.
  • Block requests over the balance? A flag, then the office looks at the hours. Usual hours matter, not just contracted:
"They cannot be punished and just given 36 hours for a week's holiday when they usually work 48."
  • Approve less than asked? Occasionally, mainly to avoid two people being off at once. They try to agree a change of dates first.
  • The journal for new requests? No: requests would bury the office's own entries ("255 holiday requests, especially when April comes around").
Reactions to the prototypes
  • Holiday Requests page: "I like that page."
  • Pleased the hours deducted can be changed on approval.
  • The "Request sent" sheet answered his next question before he asked it.
  • Confirmed they split holidays across pay periods by hand today, so the per-day breakdown helps.
  • Wants "Move to unassigned" as the default when visits overlap the holiday (the live product defaults to "Keep assigned").
  • Would like new requests visible outside Schedule: a dashboard count, or a count visible from any page.
Today
  • Requests arrive by email to a shared holidays inbox, handled by two coordinators.
  • Open to more sessions.

OakShed Care

21 October 2026

Registered Manager. Hours-based holiday schemes. One person handles all requests.

Answers to our questions
  • Block requests over the balance? Yes.
  • Response time: the office must respond within a week.
  • Email settings: a no-reply sender is fine. Filtering by territory and one vs. several recipients don't apply, since she handles every request.
  • Page access: Care Manager and above (the minimum role for PASS web anyway).
Reactions to the prototypes
  • Web and app both clear and "self-explanatory".
  • Valued the Submitted column: they approve first come, first served. The prototype now shows the time as well as the date.
  • Several employees can want the same dates; the Awaiting approval filter was felt to be enough for now.
Today
  • They check an employee's holiday history by looking through the rostering calendar.
Follow-up answers by email (29 October 2026)
  • Working out the deduction: a week is 37.5 hours spread over 5 days, so a day is 7.5 hours. The other 2 days are rest days, since most staff work 5 days a week.
  • Set days? No, care workers' working days vary from week to week.
  • The office checking the balance on approval instead of the app blocking: "Yes, that's not a problem."
  • Approving fewer days than asked: frequently, especially for more than 2 weeks (the maximum in their leave policy), or when the maximum number of people are already on leave for that period.
  • Note or conversation? They'd normally add a note "so that there is a record".

Competitor research

How comparable HR, rota and home-care products work out what a leave request costs, since PASS doesn't know which days a carer would have worked. Researched 26 October 2026 from public help centres, not hands-on use.

ApproachProductsHow the cost is foundNeeds
Working patternBrightHR, Breathe, Sage HRCounts only the days in the employee's set patternA fixed pattern per employee
RotaRotaCloudSkips days marked non-working on the rota; hours entered on the requestA published rota
AveragesDeputy, BirdieAverage hours per day (and days per week) over the last 52 weeksPay history

1From a set working pattern

Each employee has a pattern of days and hours. A request only counts the days they normally work, so the exact cost is known at request time.

BrightHR

Checks the employee's working pattern and records only the time they'd normally work. Someone on Monday, Wednesday and Friday who books Monday to Friday is charged 3 days, not 5. (Example from the Australian help centre; UK behaviour not separately documented.)

Breathe

A working pattern is required for every employee. Leave already booked is recalculated if the pattern changes.

"…so the system knows how many hours a week they work and on what days."

For flexible or zero-hours staff, the admin ticks "ignore working pattern" and the employee types the hours on each request.

Sage HR

Working-pattern policies deduct either whole days or exact working hours, depending on how the policy is set up.

2From the rota

The rota decides which days count.

RotaCloud

Days marked non-working on the rota aren't deducted. For staff on an hours allowance, the employee can suggest hours on the request (daily or a total), and the approver can edit them before approving. This is close to our hours-scheme form.

Blocking: day allowances always block a request over the balance; hour allowances only block when the balance is zero or below, the same rule the PASS app uses.

3From averages

Closest to PASS, which already uses a 52-week average for holiday pay.

Deputy

Works out an average working day from the last 52 weeks of paid work (up to 104 if needed), starting after 5 approved timesheets. Managers see the figure on the request but can't edit it.

"The amount shown when the request is approved is an estimate until the day the leave is taken."

UK rules apply the average on the first day of leave, so it can change between approval and the holiday.

Birdie Home-care competitor

Same 12.07% accrual as PASS. Each day of holiday is charged at the carer's rolling average hours per day. For bookings of 7 days or more, it uses the carer's average days worked per week, so it never needs to know which particular days they'd have worked. Part days count as full days.

Not stated: how a short booking that includes a day off is handled.

Nothing public was found for Nourish or CareLineLive.

What this means for PASS

  • No one gets an exact figure without a working pattern.Without one, products either use averages or let a person enter the amount. That supports the office confirming the amount, which customers also asked for.
  • Birdie's 7-day rule is worth exploring in discovery.PASS already works out average daily hours for holiday pay. Average days per week would give a reasonable estimate for holidays of a week or more, without the rota or a pattern. Short bookings are the hard part.
  • If we show an estimate, call it one.Deputy labels its figure an estimate even though it's calculated from data.
  • Ticked working days match the market norm.The planned change to the contract's days-per-week option (ticking which days someone works) is how BrightHR, Breathe and Sage HR solve it.
