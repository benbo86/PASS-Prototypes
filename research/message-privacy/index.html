Customer discovery

Feedback received in November 2026 from one customer, also logged as the support case below. Treat the themes as a starting point, not a pattern.

What we've heard so far

  • A carer can believe they're messaging a manager directlyA message to the office can be seen by more than one office user. That matches how permissions work today, but it isn't what a carer may expect.
  • Some conversations need to stay privateThe examples given were welfare or wellbeing concerns, personal issues affecting work, grievances or complaints, and sensitive HR conversations.

Sessions

ISSAC, Care Co-ordinator

November 2026

Written feedback on the new two-way messaging, not a prototype session. They support the feature overall.

Concern
  • When a carer sends a message to the office, several office users can potentially see it.
  • This works as designed and follows the current permissions model, but the customer sees a gap between what a carer expects and what happens.
  • A carer may want to speak privately to a specific manager or supervisor about a sensitive matter, and may think "Office" is that person.
Examples they gave
  • Welfare or wellbeing concerns.
  • Personal issues affecting work.
  • Grievances or complaints.
  • Sensitive HR-related conversations.

What this means for the prototypes

  • Say who can see a message.The web prototype already tells the sender when they compose or reply. The app now does the same: a note under "To: Office" on a new message, and a reminder above the reply box in a conversation.
  • The customer is asking for a private route, not just a notice.The case asks for one-to-one messages between a carer and a named office user, kept private from the wider office. A notice stops people expecting privacy, but it doesn't give them somewhere private to go. A private route would change who can see a conversation and how replies are routed, so it isn't in the prototypes yet.
  • Not yet known.Whether other customers want this, and whether they'd rather have a private route in messaging or handle these conversations elsewhere.

Feedback is summarised from a written note, not a transcript.

Support cases

ISSAC Case 00224696 · November 2026 · MRR £600.60

Asks for private one-to-one messaging between carers and specific office users, so a sensitive conversation isn't visible to the wider office. Named recipients included care managers, registered managers, supervisors, designated office contacts and super users.

The customer wants conversations to stay private between the sender and the person they chose, for sensitive personal matters, welfare concerns, employment issues and confidential conversations, while keeping audit controls and safeguarding requirements in place.
